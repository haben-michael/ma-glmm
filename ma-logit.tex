1. introduction: (skip generic metaanalysis material). since stijnen
2010, glmm have become the preferred model for random effects
meta-analysis based on count data. cochran etc have recommended
... . cite elsewhere for advantages over inverse variance model. while
the glmm approach avoids many of the dubious assumptions of the
inverse-variance approach, inference is still based on a chi squared
asymptotic expansion. therefore as in the IV case the conclusions based
on smaller meta-analyses are suspect. bartlett correction leverages
modelling assumptions to improve the efficiency of the testing
procedure. when the modelling assumptions are met, the imrpovement is
typically a jump in the rate of convergence from 1/n to 1/n2. [[the key
modelling assumption in this setting is the gaussianity of the random
effect.]]

while bartlett corrections were long ago applied to glm's, there do
not appear to be extensions to even the simple glmm's that are used
in meta-analysis. the reason for the reluctance appears to be
computational difficulty [[cite hanada]]. unlike glms closed form
expressions are unavailable for glmm corrections.  we argue that with
careful design these difficulties can be overcome and consumer-grade
hardware is sufficient for practical implementation.

paper outline. give stifjnen's glmm approach as
background, then describe algo. next look at sims, focusing on
robustness. for data analysis walk through the 4 stijnen analyses.
